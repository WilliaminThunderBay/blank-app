# Illustrative IM/IT Strategy (2026-2029)

## Purpose
Provide a transparent example of how a newly formed IM/IT directorate could translate organizational priorities into technology, information-governance, cybersecurity, service and capacity-building outcomes.

## Strategic pillars

### 1. Information governance by design
- Establish accountable information owners and stewards.
- Define classification, access, retention and approved-use requirements.
- Include cultural, rights-holder, consent and partner considerations in decision stage gates.
- Maintain auditable decisions and periodic access reviews.

### 2. Secure and resilient technology
- Strengthen identity, MFA and privileged-access controls.
- Establish vulnerability, patch and configuration-management baselines.
- Test backup restoration and recovery objectives.
- Improve observability and incident/problem management.

### 3. Enterprise architecture and integration
- Maintain a current application, data and integration inventory.
- Define target-state architecture principles.
- Reduce unnecessary silos using governed integration patterns.
- Preserve purpose-specific governance and security boundaries.

### 4. Digital transformation and service delivery
- Prioritize initiatives by mandate impact, risk, readiness and total cost.
- Pair technology changes with change management, training and support.
- Measure adoption and service outcomes after launch.

### 5. Capacity, relationships and accountability
- Use structured portfolio reviews and executive briefings.
- Maintain vendor scorecards, knowledge-transfer requirements and service expectations.
- Engage partners early and document co-developed decisions.
